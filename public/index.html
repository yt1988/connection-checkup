                        Help

                        What does Network X-Ray test?

                        It runs eight 1ms.dog tools one after another on this page and puts their results into one report: Who Are You (what websites can tell about you), IP Quality Test, EgressLens (which exit IP each site sees), IPv6 Test, DNS Test, the Streaming & AI Unlock Check, the 1ms.dog service latency test and the Speed Test.

                        Each tool runs in its own small window, shows its own progress and result, and passes the numbers to this page. Nothing is measured twice or simplified: the numbers here are exactly what each tool shows.

                        Why three steps and about 90 seconds?

                        A speed test fills your bandwidth and would throw off the latency and DNS measurements, so it runs last, on its own. Step 1 tests identity, IP, exits, IPv6 and DNS together. Step 2 tests streaming and AI access and service latency. Step 3 tests speed.

                        Each step waits up to 90 seconds for its tools to report back. A tool that hasn't by then is marked as not finished and left out of the grade.

                        How is the grade worked out?

                        The report has five parts, each graded A+ to F. Weights: IP quality 25%, streaming and AI 20%, speed 20%, identity and privacy 20%, connectivity (IPv6, DNS, service latency) 15%. The overall grade is the weighted average of the parts that finished. Parts that didn't finish are left out, and the report says so.

                        Tools that give their own grade (IP quality, IPv6) keep it. The rest are scored on what was measured: how many services work, how fast downloads are, how much latency rises under load, whether anything leaks or doesn't add up, and how quick DNS and common services are.

                        What are combined findings?

                        Some problems only show when you put several tools' results side by side. For example: a data center IP with a system time zone from another country, which gives a proxy away; every AI service blocked because the exit is in a restricted region; split routing sending AI and streaming through different countries; a DNS resolver in a different place from the exit IP; latency spiking during downloads. These go at the top of the report.

                        Does my data get sent anywhere?

                        The tools run in your browser just as they do on their own. This page only receives their results through the browser's built-in messaging between windows, and only from the tools it opened itself.

                        The share link is made in your browser. It holds the grades, key numbers and findings, plus each tool's own result link. IP addresses are masked the way each tool masks them by default, so they never appear in full. When you make a short link on 1ms.dog, the short link and its preview image are stored by the link service, as the share dialog explains.

                        How is this different from scripts like IPQuality or NodeQuality?

                        Those scripts run on a server over SSH, so they can only test the server's own IP. Network X-Ray runs in your browser and tests the connection you're actually using: home broadband, mobile data, a proxy or VPN. It also sees things a server script can't, like your time zone, language and WebRTC. And you get a link and an image card to share.
